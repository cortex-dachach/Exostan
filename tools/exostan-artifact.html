    How Exostan learns

    Every saved case is stored without patient identity. When you record the surgical outcome (actual difficulty, CBCT result, nerve outcome), Exostan compares it with its prediction. Once there are 5 outcomes it adds a similar-case estimate to the difficulty score. Once there are 20 CBCT-verified cases it refits the IAN weights on your own data. Rules and literature weights stay in charge until the data is large enough.

    Decision support prototype. Angulation, depth and ramus relation are measured from verified landmarks and scored with the Pederson index. IAN likelihood combines root to canal distance with Rood and Shehab signs weighted by published likelihood ratios. A panoramic radiograph cannot rule out nerve contact; CBCT is the reference standard. The treating dentist remains responsible for every clinical decision.
